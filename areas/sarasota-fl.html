What's Different About Building in Sarasota

Who issues your permit depends on your address. Properties inside Sarasota city limits are generally permitted through the City of Sarasota. Properties in unincorporated areas go through Sarasota County. Many addresses that say "Sarasota" are actually outside city limits, and some near the county line are in Manatee County, which handles its own permits through Manatee County Government. We confirm the right office for your lot before any design work is final. For current requirements, check with the building department that covers your property.

Flood zones affect the design. Much of Sarasota sits low and close to the bay and the Gulf. Your FEMA flood zone can shape the floor elevation, the foundation type, the materials used below the flood level and how an addition connects to the existing house. In flood zones, a large remodel or addition may also require bringing an older home up to current flood standards. Your local building department decides when that applies. We look at this early so it doesn't catch you off guard halfway through the project.

Wind and coastal exposure matter. All construction has to meet the Florida Building Code, which includes wind-load requirements for roofs, windows, doors and connections. Coastal properties can face extra requirements. We build to the approved plans, and when the structural design calls for it, a licensed engineer handles that part.

HOA and condo review, where it applies. Many Sarasota neighborhoods have homeowners or condominium associations that review exterior changes. If yours does, plan for that approval along with the building permit. We can help you prepare what the association asks for.

Questions Sarasota Homeowners Ask

How do I check that a contractor is licensed in Florida?

Search the contractor's name or license number on the Florida DBPR license lookup. Our certified building contractor license is CBC1261852. You should also ask any contractor for proof of insurance before signing.

How much will my remodel or addition cost?

It depends on the size, the finishes, the site, flood zone requirements and the engineering involved. We don't guess at a number. We give you a written estimate after we have seen the property and understand the scope.

How long will my project take?

The schedule depends on design time, permit review, the size of the job, inspections and the weather. We go over the main schedule factors with you at the start and keep you posted as the work moves forward.

Will my insurance cover storm or water damage repairs?

Coverage depends on your policy, and we don't decide what a policy covers, so check with your insurer or agent directly. We can document the damage and do the repair work. To prepare for storm season, the Florida Division of Emergency Management has useful resources.

What's Different About Building in Parrish

Permits usually go through Manatee County. Parrish is an unincorporated community, not a city with its own building department. For most Parrish addresses, permits, plan review and inspections are handled by Manatee County's building and development services. Rules and fees change, and some properties near jurisdiction lines may be treated differently. Always confirm current requirements with the county for your exact address. We handle this process for our clients and are familiar with the county's permitting steps.

Flood zones aren't only a coastal issue. Parrish sits inland, but the area has rivers, creeks, wetlands and low ground. Some lots fall in flood zones that can affect elevation, foundation design and drainage. We check the flood designation early and design to meet it. For questions about flood insurance or elevation requirements, talk to your insurer and the county.

Wind design still applies. Being away from the Gulf doesn't exempt a Parrish home from Florida's wind-load requirements. Windows, doors, roof connections and structural details have to meet the current Florida Building Code. Your plans and the county inspectors will check this.

HOAs and community review. Many newer Parrish neighborhoods have homeowners associations with their own architectural review. If yours does, you'll usually need their approval before work on additions, exterior changes or driveways can start, and the county permit is separate. We help you prepare drawings for both.

Rural lots bring their own questions. On acreage, site access, soil conditions and existing utilities like well and septic all affect the plan. We look at these during the first site visit, not after the design is done.

Common Questions From Parrish Homeowners

Do I get my permit from a city or the county?

For most Parrish addresses it's Manatee County, because Parrish isn't an incorporated city. Confirm with the county for your specific property. As your contractor, we normally pull the permits.

How do I check that a contractor is licensed?

Look up the license number on the Florida DBPR license search. Ours is CBC1261852.

How long will my addition or new home take?

It depends on the design, county review, HOA approval if you have one, material availability and weather. We'll explain the schedule factors for your project in your written estimate instead of guessing up front.

Can you build on a rural lot without city utilities?

Often, yes. Well, septic, access and drainage all have to be planned and approved, so we review them during the site visit and design stage.

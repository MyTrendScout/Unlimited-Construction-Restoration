Anna Maria Island Building Contractor: Homes, Remodels, Additions

Serving Anna Maria Island and nearby communities

Unlimited Construction & Restoration is a licensed and insured Florida building contractor (CBC1261852) that designs and builds new homes, remodels and additions for homeowners and property owners on Anna Maria Island. We're based on the mainland in Bradenton, a short drive from the island. We're a locally rooted owner-and-crew operation, not a franchise, and we know what it takes to build on a barrier island where flood and wind rules shape almost every decision.

What We Build and Fix on Anna Maria Island

Our main focus is design-build: we plan the project and build it under one contract, so the design is made with real construction conditions in mind from the first sketch. On Anna Maria Island that usually means:

  • New homes, including elevated homes designed around current flood requirements
  • Remodeling: kitchens, baths, layout changes and full interior renovations for full-time residents and owners of seasonal homes
  • Additions that add living space while staying within flood, setback and lot coverage limits
  • Storm and water damage restoration after Gulf weather, done to FEMA flood zone standards
  • Concrete, masonry and flatwork, such as driveways and other flatwork
  • Select commercial projects for island property owners

What's Different About Building on Anna Maria Island

Three cities, three building departments. The island isn't a single town. It is divided among the City of Anna Maria, the City of Holmes Beach and the City of Bradenton Beach, and each one runs its own permitting and zoning. The office that reviews your project depends on your address. Each city has its own rules on things like building height, lot coverage and short-term rental use. We confirm which department has authority before design gets far, and we recommend you check current requirements with that city's building department. For countywide resources, Manatee County Government is a useful starting point.

Flood zones are the main factor. Much of the island lies in FEMA-designated flood zones, and some properties near the Gulf fall in coastal zones with stricter foundation and elevation standards. These rules affect how high a new home sits, what can be built below it, and which materials can be used. On an existing home, a large enough remodel or repair can trigger federal substantial improvement rules, which may require the whole structure to meet current flood standards. We'll explain how these rules could affect your plans early on. Your local floodplain administrator makes the final call.

Wind and salt air. Homes here are built to the wind-resistance standards of the Florida Building Code, and the coastal exposure is hard on fasteners, windows, doors and finishes. We pick materials suited to salt air and plan for the extra wear it causes. Structural design for elevated and coastal homes is done by a licensed engineer, and we coordinate with them throughout.

Beachfront rules. Properties close to the beach may also need state review under Florida's Coastal Construction Control Line program, on top of city permits. Your city's building department can tell you whether your lot is affected. If it might be, we'll flag it at the start so it doesn't surprise you later.

Island logistics. Bridge access, narrow streets, tight lots and close neighbors all affect how materials get delivered and staged. We plan deliveries and site work with that in mind.

How a Project With Us Works

  1. Site visit and conversation. We walk the property, listen to your goals and look at the lot, the existing structure and anything that could limit the design.
  2. Design and engineering. We develop the plan and bring in a licensed engineer where the work needs one, keeping flood, wind and city requirements in view the whole time.
  3. Written estimate. You get a detailed written estimate. Costs and schedules on the island depend on the scope, the foundation type, flood requirements, materials and permit review, so we explain each of those factors rather than guess at a number.
  4. Permits. We prepare and submit permit applications to the right city and answer reviewers' questions.
  5. Construction and inspections. Our crew builds the project and schedules the required inspections, and we keep you updated as the work moves along.
  6. Walkthrough and closeout. We go through the finished work with you and close out the permit.

We work in English and Spanish (Hablamos español) with homeowners, subcontractors, suppliers and inspectors, so communication stays clear.

Common Questions From Anna Maria Island Homeowners

Who issues my building permit?

That depends on which of the island's three cities your property is in: Anna Maria, Holmes Beach or Bradenton Beach. Each has its own building department. We confirm the right office for your address before we submit anything.

Can I remodel my older home without elevating it?

Sometimes. It depends on the property's flood zone, its current elevation, and how the cost of the work compares to the value of the structure under substantial improvement rules. Your city's floodplain administrator decides. We can help you understand your options before you commit to a design.

How do I check that a contractor is licensed?

Look up any contractor's license on the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation's license verification site. Our certified building contractor license is CBC1261852.

Do you handle storm and water damage repairs?

Yes. We repair storm and water damage to FEMA flood zone standards. For coverage questions, talk with your insurer directly. We'll document the damage and build the repair to code.

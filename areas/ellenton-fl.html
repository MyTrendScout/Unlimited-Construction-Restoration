What's Different About Building in Ellenton

Permits come from Manatee County. Ellenton is an unincorporated community, so it has no city building department of its own. Building permits and inspections for Ellenton addresses generally go through Manatee County. If you're unsure about a property near a city boundary, check with Manatee County Government before you plan around it. We pull permits as part of every job that needs them, and our hands-on experience with Manatee County permitting helps us prepare what reviewers expect to see.

Flood zones vary by address. Ellenton sits along the north side of the Manatee River, with areas near the river and other low-lying ground. One street can be in a FEMA flood zone while the next one isn't. Your flood zone can affect the required floor elevation for new homes and additions. On existing homes, a large remodel or addition can also trigger stricter "substantial improvement" rules, based largely on the cost of the work compared with the home's value. We check this early so it doesn't surprise you halfway through design. The county building department can confirm the current requirements for your address, and your insurer is the right place for flood insurance questions.

Wind and storm design matter. Permitted work in Ellenton must meet the Florida Building Code, including its wind requirements for this part of the Gulf Coast. That affects roof connections, window and door ratings, and how new framing ties into old. When a design calls for structural engineering, we work with a licensed engineer instead of guessing.

HOAs, where they apply. Some Ellenton subdivisions have homeowners associations with their own design rules for exteriors, additions or driveways. If yours does, it's smart to get association approval alongside the county permit. We can help you put together what the HOA asks for.

How a Project With Us Works

  1. Free estimate and site visit. We walk the property, listen to what you want, and look at what affects cost and schedule: site conditions, flood zone, structure and finish choices.
  2. Design and written estimate. For design-build work, we develop plans and a written estimate together. That way the design stays tied to a budget you've actually seen.

Good communication runs through every step. We work with homeowners, subs, suppliers and inspectors in English and Spanish (hablamos español).

Common Questions From Ellenton Homeowners

Do I need a county permit for a remodel in Ellenton?

Many remodels do, especially work that involves structure, electrical, plumbing or mechanical systems. Because Ellenton is unincorporated, permits generally come from Manatee County. The county building department can confirm what your specific project requires.

How do I check that a contractor is licensed?

You can look up any Florida contractor's license through the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation. We hold Certified Building Contractor license CBC1261852.

How long will my addition or new home take?

It depends on the size of the project, design time, permit review, inspections, weather and material availability. We go over the expected schedule in your written estimate and keep you posted if anything changes.

Can I build an addition if my lot is in a flood zone?

Often, yes. Flood zone rules can affect how high the addition has to be built, and a large project can bring more of the home under stricter requirements. We look at your flood zone at the start, design around it, and confirm the current rules with Manatee County.

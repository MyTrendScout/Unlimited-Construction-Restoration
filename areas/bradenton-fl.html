What's Different About Building in Bradenton

City or county permits. Who issues your permit depends on your address. If the property is inside Bradenton city limits, the City of Bradenton handles permits and inspections. If it has a Bradenton mailing address but sits in unincorporated Manatee County, which many do, the permit goes through Manatee County's building department. We confirm the jurisdiction at the start of every project, because it affects which reviews, inspections and local rules apply.

Flood zones. Bradenton sits along the Manatee River and close to Sarasota Bay and the Gulf, so a lot of properties fall in mapped flood zones. Your flood zone can affect how high the finished floor has to sit, how an addition or substantial remodel must be built, and what materials can go below certain elevations. Large renovations on older homes in flood zones may trigger extra requirements. We look at this early, before design, so it doesn't turn up as a surprise halfway through.

Wind and coastal conditions. Every project has to meet the Florida Building Code, which includes wind-load requirements for roofs, windows, doors and connections. Salt air, heavy summer rain and humidity also shape what we recommend for materials and drainage. When a project needs structural design, we work with licensed engineers. The building department has the final word on current code specifics.

HOAs. Some Bradenton neighborhoods have homeowners associations with their own design review, and many don't. If yours does, plan for HOA approval alongside the permit. We can help you put together what they typically ask for.

How a Project With Us Works

  1. First conversation and site visit. We walk the property, listen to what you want, and look at existing conditions, flood zone status and access.
  2. Design and scope. For design-build projects, we develop plans with you and refine them until the layout, finishes and budget line up.
  3. Written estimate. You get a detailed written estimate. Cost depends on size, materials, site conditions, engineering and permit requirements. We explain each of those so you know where your money is going.
  4. Permits. We prepare and submit permit documents to the right office, city or county, and handle any review comments.
  5. Construction. Our crew does the work and coordinates subcontractors, suppliers and inspections. You'll get regular updates as work moves along.
  6. Final inspection and walkthrough. We close out the permit, walk the finished project with you, and take care of any punch-list items.

Common Questions From Bradenton Homeowners

Do I need a permit for my project in Bradenton?

Most new construction, additions, structural changes, and electrical, plumbing and mechanical work need a permit, and so do many concrete and exterior projects. Requirements vary by scope and jurisdiction, so check with the City of Bradenton or Manatee County building department. We handle permitting on our projects.

How long will my remodel or addition take?

That depends on the scope, the design, permit review times, material availability and weather. We'll give you a realistic schedule in writing once the scope is set, and we'll keep you updated if anything changes.

Can you help after storm or water damage?

Yes. We repair and rebuild storm- and water-damaged homes and businesses and build to flood zone requirements where they apply. For coverage questions, talk directly with your insurance company. For storm preparation resources, the Florida Division of Emergency Management is a good place to start.

How do I know you're properly licensed and insured?

Look up our license number, CBC1261852, on the state's DBPR site and ask us for current insurance certificates. We're also rated A+ by the BBB.

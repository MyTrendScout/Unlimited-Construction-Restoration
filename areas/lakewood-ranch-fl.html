What's Different About Building in Lakewood Ranch

Permits depend on which county you're in. Lakewood Ranch isn't an incorporated city, so it doesn't have a city building department. The community spans Manatee and Sarasota counties, and your permits come from the county your property sits in. Homes on the Manatee side go through Manatee County's building and permitting services. Homes on the Sarasota side go through Sarasota County. We check the address first, before drawings are finished, so the plans are prepared for the right office.

HOA and design review usually come first. Most Lakewood Ranch neighborhoods have a homeowners association with an architectural or modification review process. For additions, exterior changes, new driveways and many outdoor projects, that approval often has to happen before the county permit. Each association sets its own guidelines on materials, colors, setbacks and submittal requirements. We help put together the documents your HOA asks for, and we build the review time into the schedule so it doesn't surprise anyone later.

Wind and water still matter inland. Lakewood Ranch is east of the coast, but it still faces Gulf Coast hurricane winds and heavy summer rain. Homes are built to the wind-load requirements of the Florida Building Code, and that affects roof connections, windows, doors and how an addition ties into the existing structure. Flood zone designations vary from lot to lot, especially near lakes, ponds and drainage areas. Check your property's flood zone with your county's building or floodplain office early, because it can affect elevation, design and insurance. Structural design questions belong with a licensed engineer, and coverage questions with your insurer.

How a Project With Us Works

  1. Free estimate and site visit. We walk the property, listen to what you want, and talk through the factors that drive cost and schedule, such as size, materials, site conditions and approvals.
  2. Design and planning. With design-build, we develop the plans with you and keep the budget in view, so the design you approve is one that can actually be built.
  3. Written estimate. You get the scope and pricing in writing before work begins. If something changes, we talk about it and put it in writing.
  4. HOA review and permits. We prepare and submit the paperwork for your association's review and for the correct county.
  5. Construction and inspections. Our crew runs the work on site, coordinates subcontractors and suppliers, and schedules the required inspections.
  6. Walkthrough and closeout. We go over the finished work with you, take care of punch-list items, and close out the permit.

All along the way, we keep you updated in plain language. Hablamos español, so we can talk with you, your family, subs and inspectors in English or Spanish.

Common Questions From Lakewood Ranch Homeowners

Do I need HOA approval before I get a permit?

Usually, yes, for exterior work, additions and many outdoor projects. Every association sets its own rules, so check your HOA's guidelines. We'll help you prepare the submittal.

Which office issues my building permit?

It depends on your address. Properties on the Manatee County side are permitted by Manatee County, and properties on the Sarasota County side are permitted by Sarasota County. Contact the building department for your county for current requirements.

How can I verify that a contractor is licensed?

Search the contractor's name or license number on the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation site. Our certified building contractor license is CBC1261852.

How long will my remodel or addition take?

That depends on the project's size, the design, material availability, HOA review, permitting and inspections. We'll walk you through the expected sequence in your written estimate and keep you updated as the work moves forward.
